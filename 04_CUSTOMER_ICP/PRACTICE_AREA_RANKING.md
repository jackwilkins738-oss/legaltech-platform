# PRACTICE AREA RANKING — PRE-TO HYPOTHESIS

## Ranking factors
- matter volume
- repetition
- email/document volume
- administrative burden
- structured stages
- chasing
- deadlines
- ability to separate legal judgement from admin
- Proclaim prevalence likelihood
- economic value of automation

## 1. Personal Injury
Likely strong initial candidate.

Why:
- high matter volumes at many specialist firms
- repeated evidence/document requests
- medical records
- insurers
- experts
- client correspondence
- staged processes
- frequent chasing
- significant admin

Risks:
- special-category health data
- limitation/deadline risk
- complex sub-types

## 2. Clinical Negligence
Very strong admin/doc burden.

Why:
- heavy medical records/evidence
- experts
- chronology
- correspondence

Risks:
- higher complexity
- lower repeatability than straightforward PI
- extremely sensitive health data

## 3. Conveyancing
Huge workflow repetition and high volume.

Why:
- document-heavy
- structured stages
- third-party chasing

Risks:
- specialist integrations
- property/funds risks
- legal-accounting/client-money exposure
- incumbent systems often specialised

## 4. Housing / Disrepair / Volume Litigation
Potentially attractive:
- volume
- repeated letters/evidence
- workflow consistency

Needs market-size validation.

## 5. Employment
Moderate:
- deadlines
- document/correspondence
but more bespoke legal strategy.

## 6. Immigration
Document intensive, but workflow/rule change complexity can be high.

## 7. Family
Less attractive as first automation wedge:
- highly sensitive
- emotionally complex
- less standardised matters
- judgement-heavy

## Provisional first segment
High-volume PI firms already running Proclaim.

Why:
- likely strong overlap between Proclaim installed base and workflow-heavy administration
- strong document/email automation opportunity
- enough firm scale for ROI

This is a hypothesis until external research + TO.
