# TIME-AND-MOTION RESEARCH DESIGN V2

## Design
Use continuous direct observation as the primary timing method, supplemented by system/report evidence for frequency and population.

## Sampling frame
Record, rather than assume:
- roles;
- practice areas;
- matter stages;
- day/time window;
- workload condition;
- case complexity;
- sample mode;
- normal vs exception path.

## Multitasking
Do not double-count elapsed time. If two meaningful tasks overlap, record the primary active task and note concurrency. If the observer cannot reliably split them, mark timing contaminated and exclude from mean duration.

## Observer effect
Record when behaviour appears altered because of observation. Do not try to “correct” mathematically from a single day; treat it as a limitation and seek system-log triangulation.

## Reliability
If multiple observers are used, double-code shared/example events before pooling results. Time-motion literature consistently treats observer reliability and taxonomy consistency as core validity requirements.

## Sample-size rule
No fixed n makes a workflow “true.” Use >=5 measured examples only as an internal provisional timing gate; increase sampling for high-variance/high-consequence workflows and stop when additional observations no longer materially change the task definition/timing range/root-cause conclusion.
