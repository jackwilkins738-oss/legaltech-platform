# VENDOR / SUPPLY-CHAIN RISK FRAMEWORK

## Principle
Using a vendor transfers operations, not accountability.

NCSC guidance says third-party supply-chain risks must be understood and managed, including what customer data and metadata suppliers can access.

## Vendor classes

### Critical
Can affect:
- confidentiality
- availability
- authentication
- production

Examples:
cloud, database, identity, Graph, AI provider.

### High
Processes customer confidential data.

### Medium
Internal company data or non-critical production support.

### Low
No sensitive/system access.

## Due diligence
For each vendor:
- company
- service
- data
- metadata
- subprocessor role
- location
- certifications
- breach history
- encryption
- access controls
- incident notification
- deletion
- backups
- availability
- subcontractors
- financial stability
- exit/export
- dependency risk

## Contract
Where risk justifies:
- security requirements
- privacy terms
- breach notice
- subprocessor restrictions
- data location
- return/deletion
- availability
- audit/assurance
- termination assistance

## Ongoing review
Critical:
annual minimum plus material event.

Triggers:
- breach
- acquisition
- major ToS change
- new subprocessor
- region change
- architecture change

## Concentration
Document:
"What happens if this vendor disappears tomorrow?"
