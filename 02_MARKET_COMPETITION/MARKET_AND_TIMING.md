# MARKET AND TIMING

## Market signals
The 2026 UK/Ireland Clio research reports:
- AI use is widespread
- 88% of legal professionals use AI
- only 27% have embedded it into daily workflows

Treat this as vendor-sponsored research, but it is strategically useful:
AI availability is no longer the central problem.
Operational integration and governance are.

## Timing thesis
The opportunity is created by five simultaneous forces:
1. Legacy PMS dissatisfaction and technical debt
2. Cloud migration
3. AI adoption
4. pressure to reduce admin/non-billable work
5. increasing security/governance requirements

## What "why now" should become
Not:
"AI is new."

Better:
"Law firms now have AI tools, but their operational systems and workflows are still fragmented, manually operated and difficult to change safely."

## Market segmentation
Prioritise firms with:
- large repetitive matter volumes
- meaningful admin headcount
- standardised processes
- Proclaim dependency
- Microsoft 365
- clear operations leadership
- enough budget for enterprise software
- enough scale that saving 15–30 minutes/user/day creates obvious ROI

## Likely attractive initial size
Hypothesis:
50–250 users may be more attractive than very small firms because:
- workflow volume is high
- administrative cost is material
- management pain is visible
- ACV can justify onboarding/security work
while still avoiding the extreme procurement complexity of the largest firms.

This must be validated.
