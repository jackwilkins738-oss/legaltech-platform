## Deep profiles

### Access Legal / Proclaim / CaseMatters Evo
The incumbent's moat is not UX; it is accumulated firm configuration. Years of fields, workflows, precedents, permissions, integrations and staff familiarity make even an unpopular system hard to replace. CaseMatters Evo attacks the obvious browser/performance/AI gap, so 'modern UI + AI' is not a sufficient differentiation. Our advantage must be measurable operational automation, much easier configuration, safer integrations, clean migration/reconciliation and exportability.

### Clio
Clio has expanded from practice management into a broad intelligent legal-work platform. Its $1bn vLex acquisition connects legal authority with live matter context, while Manage AI/Vincent adds drafting, research, deadline extraction, recommendations and court/docket workflows. The strategic lesson is that the winner owns both the system of record and the context used to execute work. We should copy the architecture idea—not attempt to recreate vLex-scale research in-house.

### Actionstep
Actionstep is a direct warning that UK midsize firms will move off legacy systems when migration is credible. It combines flexible workflows, accounting and cloud deployment, then layers firmwide intelligence on top. Its UK growth and research into midsize firms validate our 50–250-user focus and show that automation, onboarding friction and financial visibility are active buying themes.

### LEAP
LEAP wins by shipping content, not just infrastructure: forms, precedents, matter types, legal accounting, client collaboration, AI and legal research. That reduces implementation burden. The lesson is to build reusable practice packs—starting with PI—containing stages, fields, document classes, tasks, workflows, KPIs and client-update templates.

### Filevine
Filevine is one of the clearest examples of vertical SaaS expansion. It owns the litigation matter and keeps adding high-friction services around it. Its 2026 medical-record partnership is especially important: record retrieval is being treated as part of the product workflow, not an external inconvenience. That strongly supports an Evidence & Records Orchestration service for us.

### Litify
Litify's category language has shifted toward a 'platform of action.' Its ACE layer combines case context, proactive recommendations, cross-matter intelligence and multi-step agents. This directly validates our exception-queue thesis: the system should continuously detect what is missing, risky or overdue and then execute permitted next steps.

Intake, treatment, records, deadlines, demands and settlement all use PI-native concepts. This means our first PI experience cannot look like a generic configurable database; it must feel immediately fluent in PI workflows.

### Smokeball
Smokeball wins by making automation passive: activity/time capture, document organisation and workflows happen with little prompting. The best automation often removes the need to remember an admin task. This supports automatic filing, activity capture, matter-health checks and default-by-exception UX.

CARET's AI is built around existing matters, time, billing and deadlines and explicitly surfaces what needs attention. This closely matches the strongest extension to our wedge: a Matter Command / Case Health layer that tells users which matters require intervention today.

### CosmoLex
CosmoLex demonstrates how sticky native legal accounting can be. It also reinforces our decision not to build it early: accounting would deepen lock-in but introduces a separate regulated operational domain. Integrate and expose financial status before attempting to become the ledger of record.

### Osprey Approach
Osprey's UK strength is not just features; current case studies emphasize usability, onboarding, migration and responsive support. For midsize UK firms, service quality is a buying factor. This supports a Legal Operations Engineering implementation layer with clear ownership rather than anonymous support queues.

### Denovo CaseLoad
Denovo combines legal accounting, matter workflow and practice-specific applications with legal research AI. The lesson is that regional and practice-specific depth creates defensibility. Our canonical model should remain generic underneath, while customer-facing practice packs become highly specific.

### Elite 3E
Elite's reported 125% YoY SaaS-user growth shows even mission-critical law-firm finance is moving to cloud. The lesson is not to fear eventual replacement of legacy cores, but to earn that right through enterprise trust, migration quality and measurable value.

### Aderant Expert Sierra
Aderant's Agent Center brings agentic automation to work-to-cash and operational workflows. At larger firms, the buyer cares less about 'case-management features' and more about profitability, cash conversion, utilisation and operational control. Cross-matter business intelligence should therefore become a later enterprise module.

### Peppermint / Litera
Peppermint's Microsoft-native approach matters because lawyers spend much of their day in Outlook, Word and Teams. A separate browser application creates context switching. Long-term, our most important actions should also be available inside Microsoft surfaces.

## What the 20 collectively show
1. **AI is becoming embedded.** Standalone chat is commoditising; winning AI has matter context, permissions, firm standards and actions.
2. **Systems of record are becoming systems of action.** The system continuously detects what changed, what is missing and what should happen next.
3. **Vertical depth beats generality.** PI specialists win by understanding records, treatment, demands, settlement and evidence—not just storing files.
4. **Workflow-adjacent services are becoming product.** Records, court filing, payments, research, forms and client communication increasingly sit inside the platform.
5. **Client experience is part of legal operations.** Portals, messaging, onboarding and e-sign are core.
6. **Firm economics are becoming connected to matter operations.** Time, billing, workload and profitability increasingly drive product value.
7. **Configuration must be easy.** Powerful but specialist-dependent configuration recreates legacy pain.
8. **Migration and implementation are strategic.** They materially affect buying decisions and switching risk.
