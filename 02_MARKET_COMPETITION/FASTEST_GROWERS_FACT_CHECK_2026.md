# FASTEST-GROWING LEGAL TECH — FACT CHECK & EVIDENCE QUALITY

## Purpose
Separate hard facts from vendor claims, reputable press reports, and third-party estimates for the companies previously identified as major growth signals.

## Evidence classes
- **A — independently reported / hard external evidence:** reputable financial/news reporting, completed financing, or externally corroborated transaction.
- **B — first-party hard metric:** company-reported ARR, customer count, financing, user count or adoption metric. Useful, but not independently audited unless explicitly stated.
- **C — vendor marketing / customer case-study metric:** directional evidence only.
- **D — third-party estimate:** never present as company-reported fact.

# Determining factors that correlate with the verified growth

## Factor 1 — They sell into high-value work
Harvey and Legora target top law firms and enterprise legal teams.
Clio spans a huge legal customer base.
Filevine targets complex case operations.

High-value users can support high ACVs.

## Factor 2 — They expand after the initial sale
Growth is not only new logos.
The platforms add:
- agents,
- AI,
- research,
- payments,
- records,
- analytics,
- additional teams.

This creates expansion revenue.

## Factor 3 — They solve a workflow, not a model-access problem
Harvey: agents.
Legora: collaborative legal work.
Clio: matter + legal intelligence.
Filevine: litigation operations.
Actionstep: cloud law-firm operations.

## Factor 4 — Implementation is part of the product
Harvey's legal engineers and Actionstep/Elite migration teams reduce adoption risk.

## Factor 5 — They become embedded in daily work
Harvey reports very high adoption in selected customer studies.
Legora describes tens of thousands of daily users.
Clio is the underlying system of record.
Filevine centralizes communications/documents/tasks.

## Factor 6 — They create proprietary context
The deeper they sit inside:
- matters,
- documents,
- workflows,
- firm standards,
- billing,
the more valuable and harder to replace they become.

## Factor 7 — They attach AI to measurable outcomes
The strongest companies increasingly measure:
- time saved,
- cases handled,
- conversion,
- billing,
- cycle time,
- productivity,
not prompt volume.

# Implication for us

The reliable evidence changes the conclusion slightly:

The goal should NOT be to imitate every company described as "fast growing."

The strongest verified winners share four traits:
1. **mission-critical workflow position**,
2. **expansion revenue inside existing customers**,
3. **deep proprietary context/data**,
4. **implementation/adoption support strong enough to move firms into new behavior**.

For us, the equivalent path is:

**Proclaim augmentation → daily exception/case-health layer → PI workflow depth → firm-specific workflow agents → records/evidence services → cross-matter intelligence → migration/replacement.**

That is better supported by the evidence than simply building a broad feature list.
