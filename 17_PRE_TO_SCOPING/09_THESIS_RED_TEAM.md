# THESIS RED TEAM

## Objective
Try to kill the thesis before customers do.

---

# Attack 1 — This is configuration failure, not product failure

## Hypothesis
Good Proclaim teams already automate these workflows.

## Test
Interview:
- skilled Proclaim developer
- well-run Proclaim firm
- Access consultant

Ask:
Can inbound filing/task creation already be automated?
How hard?
Cost?
Maintenance?

## If true
Position product on:
- faster configuration,
- better AI understanding,
- cross-system operations,
not "Proclaim can't do it."

---

# Attack 2 — CaseMatters Evo fixes the problem

## Hypothesis
Evo modernises UI/performance/automation enough to eliminate wedge.

## Test
Get real Evo demos/users.
Compare:
- inbound automation
- exception management
- configuration
- email handling
- AI
- migration.

## If true
Either integrate Evo or move toward a workflow Evo still does poorly.

---

# Attack 3 — Microsoft Copilot commoditises the wedge

## Hypothesis
Copilot can classify/summarise/draft Outlook mail cheaply.

## Counter-question
Can it:
- matter-match securely,
- apply firm workflows,
- write to Proclaim,
- reconcile,
- audit,
- manage exceptions,
- track cross-matter operations?

## If Microsoft closes these gaps
Our moat must move deeper into legal workflow/Proclaim/practice packs.

---

# Attack 4 — Firms won't grant mailbox/API access

## Test
External interviews.

## If true
Possible alternatives:
- customer-hosted connector
- user-triggered add-in
- read-only ingest
- SharePoint/drop-folder wedge

But this can kill current economics.

---

# Attack 5 — Human review wipes out savings

## Test
Measure exception rate.

Example:
AI saves 3 min/event but 60% require 2 min review.
Net saving may be too small.

## Kill rule
Do not claim ROI until net human minutes are measured.

---

# Attack 6 — Error consequences are too high

Potential:
- wrong matter filing
- wrong client send
- missed deadline

## Mitigation
Start with:
- proposals
- internal reversible actions
- conservative thresholds

If useful automation cannot occur without unacceptable risk, change wedge.

---

# Attack 7 — Services destroy SaaS margin

## Hypothesis
Every firm needs custom workflows and integration consulting.

## Test
Track implementation hours/customer and reuse %.

## Kill/pivot threshold
If product cannot reach high configuration reuse after several firms, narrow ICP/workflow.

---

# Attack 8 — PI is too crowded

Competitors:
Filevine, Neos, SmartAdvocate, Litify.

## Counter
UK Proclaim installed base + legacy augmentation may be white space.

## Test
Ask UK PI firms whether US PI products are credible alternatives.

---

# Attack 9 — Startup procurement makes enterprise sales impossible

## Test
Ask security/procurement leaders:
minimum certifications?
insurance?
trading history?
pen test?
ISO?

## Response
Start controlled pilot with limited data/access if accepted.

If nobody will pilot pre-certification, adjust target size.

---

# Attack 10 — Admin savings don't create cash value

Capacity savings may not reduce payroll.

## Test
Look for:
- avoided hires
- growth without headcount
- backlog reduction
- more cases per handler
- faster settlement/revenue.

Selling "time saved" alone is weaker.

---

# Attack 11 — Full PMS replacement is unnecessary

This may still be a very large company as an operations layer.

Do not force replacement merely because it was original ambition.

---

# Attack 12 — Incumbents copy us

Features can be copied.

Harder assets:
- workflow/eval dataset
- Proclaim adapter depth
- deployment speed
- practice packs
- migration engine
- benchmarks
- customer trust.

If moat is only AI prompts, thesis is weak.
