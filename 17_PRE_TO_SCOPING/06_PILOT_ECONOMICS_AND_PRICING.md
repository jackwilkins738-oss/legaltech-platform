# PILOT ECONOMICS & PRICING MODEL

## Goal
Know which workflows can support meaningful commercial pricing before selling them.

---

# Value model

## Annual Capacity Value
Annual Human Hours Saved × Loaded Hourly Cost

## Cash Saving
Only count cost actually removed:
- avoided hire
- contractor reduction
- overtime
- vacancy not backfilled
- external service cost

Do not call redeployed staff time a cash saving.

## Other measurable value
- shorter case cycle
- increased cases/FTE
- lower backlog
- faster client response
- fewer rework events
- lower supervision time

Keep separate unless monetization is defensible.

# Pilot price scenarios

## £5k pilot
Appropriate if:
- narrow workflow
- light integration
- founder-heavy validation
- modest value uncertainty

Target customer annualized value:
ideally £20k+.

## £10k pilot
Appropriate if:
- real Graph + Proclaim integration
- 10–30 users
- measurable workflow

Target annualized value:
£40k+.

## £15k pilot
Appropriate if:
- meaningful implementation
- high-volume team
- baseline + reporting
- deeper integration/security review

Target annualized value:
£60k+.

## £25k pilot
Only if:
- large team
- high measured value
- enterprise integration work
- pilot itself delivers operational benefit

Target annualized value:
£100k+.

# Annual ACV scenarios

## £20k ACV
Need at least ~£60k of believable annual value for comfortable 3x ROI.

## £40k ACV
Need ~£120k value.

## £60k ACV
Need ~£180k value.

## £100k ACV
Need ~£300k value.

This is a sales-value heuristic, not a universal rule.

---

# Example

100 staff
× 15 min/day saved
× 220 working days
= 330,000 minutes
= 5,500 hours

At £25 loaded hourly cost:
£137,500 capacity value.

Potential:
£30k–£50k ACV can be rational if savings are real, adopted and attributable.

But:
if only 30% of predicted time saving materializes:
£41,250 value.

Therefore pilots must measure reality.

---

# Pilot cost model

Track our costs:
- engineering hours
- implementation hours
- customer success
- AI
- OCR
- infrastructure
- support
- security/procurement work

If every £10k pilot consumes £20k internal labour, the business is not yet scalable.

---

# Pricing hypothesis

Initial likely structure:
- implementation/pilot fee
- base annual platform
- tier by users / workflow volume / module
- optional migration/services

Avoid:
- pure per-token pricing
- cheap per-seat SaaS positioning
- bespoke consultancy invoices forever

---

# Conversion decision

Pilot converts only if:
- measurable operational value
- acceptable safety
- users adopt
- integration works
- support burden manageable
- customer accepts credible annual price

Do not convert a bad pilot merely to get ARR.
