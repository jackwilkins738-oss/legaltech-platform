# CHANGE MANAGEMENT & ADOPTION

## Why this is a product requirement
Legal AI projects fail when workflow does not change, even if the model works.

Thomson Reuters' 2026 legal research reports:
- 50% of law-firm professionals say strategy is not visible in day-to-day work;
- 39% say people are not equipped to work in the intended way;
- 34% report using AI tools not authorised by their firm;
- firms with a named AI strategy report materially better perceived value.

Sources:
- https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/future-of-professionals-2026/report-legal
- https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/future-of-professionals-2026/report

## Our adoption model

### Phase 0 — baseline
Measure current:
- time/task;
- clicks;
- workarounds;
- backlog;
- errors;
- confidence.

### Phase 1 — champions
For each pilot team:
- 1 operational champion;
- 1 legal supervisor;
- 1 IT/apps contact.

Champion is not unpaid support. Their role is feedback and social proof.

### Phase 2 — role-specific training
Do not train everyone on "AI".

Admin:
- review queue;
- correction;
- escalation.

Fee earner:
- source verification;
- approval;
- legal judgement boundary.

Team leader:
- exceptions;
- case health;
- queue management.

IT/security:
- connector health;
- permissions;
- audit.

### Phase 3 — controlled launch
Start with:
- one workflow;
- one team;
- one or few mailboxes;
- clear reversible actions.

### Phase 4 — adoption measurement
Track:
- weekly active users;
- review completion;
- override/correction rate;
- time/event;
- % work bypassing platform;
- support tickets;
- user-reported trust.

## Adoption risk taxonomy
- resistance because workflow is slower;
- fear of job loss;
- lack of trust;
- unclear accountability;
- too many false positives;
- duplicate systems;
- manager not using dashboards;
- shadow AI.

## Design responses
- show provenance;
- allow correction;
- never hide automation;
- reduce clicks below current workflow;
- visible ownership;
- measurable benefit per role.

## Training standard
Every user should understand:
1. what system can do;
2. what it cannot;
3. what requires verification;
4. how to report error;
5. how data is handled;
6. who remains accountable.

## Pilot adoption gate
Do not call pilot successful solely on technical accuracy.

Target evidence:
- >70% intended-user weekly adoption;
- falling handling time;
- stable correction rate;
- no material safety incident;
- users choose platform over workaround.

Exact thresholds become customer-specific after baseline.
